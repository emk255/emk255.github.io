kim.elliot@northeastern.edu

Hi! I’m Elliot, a first-year PhD student in Computer Science at Northeastern University, advised by Tina Eliassi-Rad.

I study why AI systems fail and how to make their behavior more interpretable and reliable. My research connects LLM evaluation, graph representations, and AI agents for scientific discovery. I am particularly interested in what shared failures across models mean for the people and scientific workflows that rely on them.

In my current work, I investigate how different representations of gene co-expression networks affect LLM agents’ scientific analysis. Previously, I studied correlated errors across LLMs, including how shared errors can systematically exclude the same applicants in hiring pipelines.

I also build research software. Working with Yuan-Sen Ting, I used coding agents to develop and validate pyKurucz, a Python reimplementation of legacy Fortran stellar-atmosphere and spectrum-synthesis codes. This work provided the foundation for follow-up astrophysics research, including Payne Zero.

Before Northeastern, I earned an M.Eng. in Computer Science and a B.A. in Computer Science and Information Science at Cornell University. I was fortunate to work with Nikhil Garg, Tong Zhang, and David Strang. I also worked as a software engineer at Commure, building backend systems for healthcare revenue cycle management.
